# Hearing Drill And Authored Arenas

## Hearing Practice

The drill deliberately uses a lightweight overhead board, not another animated
3D scene. The player listens to hidden native shots or four footsteps, estimates
the world location, and gets angular/distance error and a score. A shuffled deck
covers eight direction sectors and three distance bands with jitter; trials span
4..24m. The actual position stays hidden until an answer.

Settings cover sample weapon, step surface, HRTF/equal-power spatialization,
headphones/stereo/mono, audio gain, optional muffling and direction-only scoring.
The default distance falloff uses extracted CS2 sound-event curves with metres
converted to Source units. An inverse-distance browser calibration option remains
available. Mono is actually downmixed and scores distance only: it cannot teach
left versus right. Calibration exposes left/right/near/far samples. Device volume
must stay fixed for meaningful distance practice; browser HRTF is not CS2 HRTF.

Audio is resumed from the user's click. Missing samples, muted output or unavailable
Web Audio cannot enable an answer. A generation gate cancels pending loads and
scheduled voices when replaying, answering, hiding the tab, opening panels,
changing settings or unmounting. History is bounded to 100 locally stored answers.
No combat XP is fabricated for this drill.

Duel AI uses the same extracted weapon-event volume and distance knots for
gunshot audibility, independently of the user's master volume. Suppressed shots
therefore no longer inherit a generic loud-rifle radius. Audibility still uses
an approximate cover multiplier and skill threshold; it is not Source 2's full
audio propagation mixer. Metadata is generated by `tools/import-audio.mjs` and
checked against the native audio manifest by `tools/check-assets.mjs`.

## Arena Construction

54 explicit object bundles are authored across freight, service, courtyard,
switchback, loading and workshop themes. Each describes a purpose, closed
collision geometry, native-size cover, decorative prop shells and a reserved
footprint. Examples include customs inspection bays, generator yards, planted
courts, vehicle chicanes, dispatch docks and tool-storage work areas.

A seed chooses assemblies and placements. Opposing assemblies are mirrored for
balance; spacing reservations prevent overlap and unusable passage slits. A main
central screen remains, while each regular map receives three opposing POI pairs.
Compact maps use two pairs. Smaller arenas change bounds and assembly count,
not player hull, prop scale, movement speed or weapon math.

Generation validates cover-return segments, navigable routes, both camp peek
directions and five separated covered spawns. Bounded candidate attempts fall
back to a deterministic validated layout. Construction runs between rounds,
not per frame. Decorative shell pieces are batched by the existing material
batcher; visual holes cannot conceal solid colliders.

One- and two-bot UI configurations permit scale 0.65, roughly 15.6x20.8m. Larger
rosters keep the minimum full-size arena. Seeded tests cover 1,680 arenas across
seven scales, all authored templates, boundaries, overlap, routes and covered
spawns.

This borrows the authored-location/placement-reservation approach rather than
trying to reproduce Valheim's world generator or copying its assets. References:
[Valheim FAQ](https://www.valheimgame.com/faq/) and the primary mod-toolkit
[Jotunn location/zone tutorial](https://valheim-modding.github.io/Jotunn/tutorials/zones.html).
